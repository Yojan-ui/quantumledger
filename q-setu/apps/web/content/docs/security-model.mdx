---
title: Security model
description: What Q-Setu defends against, what it doesn't, and what it relies on.
---

## Defends against

- **Harvest now, decrypt later.** Recorded traffic and stolen backups stay sealed against a future
  quantum computer (hybrid ML-KEM, per-record wrapped keys).
- **Forgery after Q-Day.** Records, certificates and log entries carry ML-DSA signatures.
- **On-path tampering and replay.** AES-GCM authentication, counters and a replay window.
- **Insiders editing stored data.** Vault signatures and the Q-Ledger hash chain detect any edit.

## Does not defend against

- A compromised endpoint: malware on a client or server sees plaintext.
- Side channels: the JavaScript implementations do not claim constant-time execution.
- Denial of service, and weaknesses in the PKI that TLS itself relies on.

## Relies on

- `@noble/post-quantum` for ML-KEM, ML-DSA and SLH-DSA. **Not independently audited** (self-audited
  by its author).
- `@noble/curves`, `@noble/hashes` and `@noble/ciphers`, which are independently audited.
- The secrecy of node identities and KMS keys. Use an HSM in production.

Full details, including what the demos simulate, are on the [security page](/security).
